# Feedback: Callum (29 Oct 2026, release 47)
**Before I started:** The brief was clear and I was comfortable with it. I had to assume a few things. My garden is about 14 × 20 m with the house at the bottom. The tunnel is 1.83 × 5.49 m (6 × 18 ft). I set the beds 1, 2, 3 and 4 as garlic; leeks and kale; potatoes next year; spare. I left the tunnel at the app's default warmth (8 °C by day, 2 °C at night), because that's about what my max-min thermometer says in spring.

I'm Callum, 52. I've grown near Aviemore, 250 m up in a frost hollow by the Spey, for twenty years. I used a Windows laptop at 1366 × 768 with a mouse. I set up from the postcode PH22 1AA and drew the garden: four 3 × 1.2 m beds, the polytunnel, two apples, rhubarb and a row of raspberries. I put in what's in the ground now (garlic, leeks, kale, winter purslane in the tunnel, and the fruit). Then I planned next year (broad beans, potatoes, tomatoes and a courgette in the tunnel) and let it draft 2027. I checked each date against my spreadsheet twice: once with the app's own frost estimate, and once with my own frost dates typed in. I also looked at Today, the Shed, the shopping list, plant pages, help, search, Settings and the ··· menus. It took about two and a half hours.

## Would I pay for it?
No, not as it is. My spreadsheet costs nothing and is right for my garden. If the dates were right for Strathspey, I'd pay a one-off £10 to £15, or £5 a year at most. I won't pay a monthly subscription for a planner I use hard in January and February and then glance at. For comparison, I pay about £30 a year for seeds from a northern supplier, because their varieties suit up here.

Before I paid, these would have to be true:
- sowing and planting dates move with my frost dates, not only the tender plant-outs;
- I can get my dates out as a CSV or print them;
- a season's real dates feed next year's plan.

What would make me cancel: one more "plant potatoes now" in April.

## Would I keep using it?
Probably not. Drawing the garden was quick, I liked giving the tunnel its own warmth, and the shopping list is handy. But this is exactly the sort of app I distrust. It found Aviemore and gave a sensible last frost (26 May, play safe 9 June, against my first week of June). Then it ignored that for almost everything: potatoes outside on 1 April, broad beans on 1 February, tomatoes sown on 1 February. When I typed in my own frost dates (5 June and 5 September), the only thing that moved was tomatoes and courgettes into the tunnel, by ten days. The help says "Your own frost dates are the best way to say so." I did, and it didn't listen. I'd keep the spreadsheet and drop this.

## What I like (keep it)
- **Greenhouse warmth you can set** (Garden, select greenhouse, Under cover). The "warmer by day / at night" numbers and the "Heated, kept frost-free" box are the right idea for a tunnel. Under the default frost estimate, tomatoes and courgettes into the tunnel on 12 May is the one date that's near-ish.
- **Editable frost dates** (Settings, Seasons and weather, Frosts), with the honest wording: "frost pockets … can be weeks either side." The 50:50 and play-safe explanation is good.
- **The last frost estimate itself**: 26 May, play safe 9 June. That's within a week of my records.
- **Sizes in mm in the side panel**. I typed 3000 × 1200 and got my beds exactly. Duplicate is quick.
- **"Already in the ground" and "What's happened?"** I could say the leeks were sown 5 April and planted out 26 June, and the timeline took it.
- **Row and Block planting** with a count. Click two ends and it worked out 21 leeks at 15 cm.
- **The shopping list** (Today, Planning ahead). It's grouped into seed, seed potatoes, compost and feed, with the bed each is for. That's the right shape.
- **The leek plant page** (Plants, Leek, late): sow March–April, plant out June–July, and "they stand all winter". That's correct for me.
- **No account, and saved on the device.** Good.

## What I don't like
1. **Sowing and planting dates aren't adjusted for the Highlands, even with my own frost dates** (planting Timeline, Garden). Potatoes are "planted about 1 Apr, up about 20 Apr" outdoors. Broad beans are "sown about 1 Feb, up 19 Feb, flowering 18 Apr". Tomatoes are "sown about 1 Feb". None of these moved when I set the last frost to 5 June. Potato shoots up on 20 April here get blackened. This is the whole job of the app, and it's the reason I'd stop.
2. **The first frost estimate is a month late and the climate is coastal** (Settings). The first frost is given as 5 October; mine is early September. "Your climate, in detail" uses Inverness, Aberdeen and Edinburgh, all near sea level. It gives January nights 1 °C and "about 280 days above 5 °C". At 250 m in a hollow, that's nonsense: my growing season is nearer 180 days. There's nowhere to say height or "frost hollow".
3. **A photo covers the planting bar** (Garden, pick a plant to plant). The season photo behind the plan sits on top of the right half of the bar. I couldn't click "Already in the ground", "Change plant" or "Done" with the mouse. My raspberries went in as "Planned" when I'd ticked them as already in. Only Fill for me, One, Row and Block respond.
4. **Kale "Finished by about 30 Oct"** (kale card) while the same card says "Harvest Sep–Mar". The draft for next year says it "is still standing until about March". Kale is the crop that carries me through a Highland winter.
5. **Today says "PLANT Garlic, hardneck in Bed 1"** after I'd marked it planted on 27 Oct. If the jobs don't believe what I've told it, I can't tick jobs off.
6. **The garlic harvest is two months early.** The card says "Ready to harvest from about 16 Jun" and "Harvest Jun–Jul". I lift in August, as I said.
7. **The plan gets a fraction of a laptop screen** (Garden). With the Plants tray open, the plan is about 935 × 300 px. The side panel takes 320 px and the timeline and tray take the bottom half. The canvas also changes size when you enter planting mode, so beds move under the mouse. My first leek row went into Bed 1 instead of Bed 2, and a second went onto the lawn.
8. **"Sow or plant now 171"** (Plants tray) on 29 October in Strathspey. That list includes blueberry, sweet pea and Californian lilac. Here, the honest answer is nearly nothing.
9. **General jobs written for the south** (Today, before any plants): "Sow overwintering broad beans outdoors" and "Lift and store dahlia tubers". Autumn broad beans don't survive here in most winters.
10. **The rotation lumps kale in with the leeks** (Today, Next year, Draft it). It moved Bed 2's leeks and kale together into Bed 4 as "Other crops → Onions". Kale is a brassica. Telling it what grew in Beds 3 and 4 in 2026 changed nothing in the draft.

## Contradictions
- **Kale:** the card says "Harvest Sep–Mar" and "Finished by about 30 Oct". The 2027 draft says "still standing until about March". Today and November list it as "Ready to pick".
- **Garlic:** marked planted on 27 Oct, yet Today and All October jobs still say "PLANT Garlic, hardneck in Bed 1".
- **Leeks:** the first harvest was 30 Oct after I entered the sowing date, then 16 Jan after I entered the plant-out date. The leeks never appear under "Ready to pick", although the card says Dec–Apr.
- **Winter purslane:** the card says "Ready to harvest from about 19 Nov", while Today lists it under "Ready to pick" now. After I marked it sown on 15 Sept in the tunnel bed, it said "Plant out now", but it's already planted where it grows.
- **Leek sowing:** marking it sown on 5 Apr showed "Running behind. Expected to be up by 19 Apr" on 29 October.
- **Frost dates:** the help says "Your own frost dates are the best way to say so", but they don't move sowing dates or potato planting.
- **Overlap warnings:** "I know, it's fine" on the leek and kale overlap came back as two warnings after the 2027 draft copied them into Bed 4.
- **Courgette:** "Polytunnel is taken until about October by cordon tomato and winter purslane", in a 10 m² tunnel with one row of tomatoes and a small block of purslane.

## What's superfluous
- **Sun cream reminders and "The UV on Today"** (Settings). In Strathspey? Hide them by default.
- **"Plant editor: show the tools for checking plant notes against their sources"** (Settings). That's a developer setting. Take it out of a user's Settings.
- **Five "Looks"** (Cottage, Heritage, Allotment, Modern, Minimal) and the photo month. I'd keep one and spend the effort on dates.
- **The season photos around the plan.** They're pretty, but they eat screen space and one blocks the planting bar. Default to "Hide photos around the plan".
- **"Your season, wrapped"**, which I couldn't open anyway. Recipes on the leek page ("Leek and potato soup, 40 min").
- **The "Getting started" checklist and "Put it on your home screen" card** on Today after setup. They're still nagging after I've drawn the whole garden.
- **"Pollinators: good for bees, July to August"** on leeks I harvest before they flower.
- **3D, In flower, Water.** I'd never use them.

## What's missing
- **Height and a frost-hollow setting**, or simply growing-season length. Or better: let me type my own date for one crop ("I plant potatoes 25 May") and shift the rest.
- **Sowing dates tied to the frost dates**, for hardy and half-hardy crops as well as tender ones. Potatoes should key off the last frost.
- **A polytunnel** as a kind, not a greenhouse renamed. Not essential, since the warmth settings do the work.
- **Export.** I found nothing to export my sowing and planting dates as CSV, and nothing to print. Search for "print" says "Nothing matches". The only way out is "Download a backup", which isn't a file I can open beside my spreadsheet.
- **Import of past years.** I have fifteen years of dates. There's no way to bring them in, or to see "last year I sowed leeks on 5 April" when planning.
- **Varieties on the shopping list**, and northern-hardy varieties (for example, early potatoes for short seasons).
- **A count for single plants on the lawn**, such as a rhubarb patch of three crowns. It's one plant per drop unless you make a bed.
- **Apple rootstock and biennial cropping.** It just says "Ready to pick" every year.

## My top five changes
1. **Shift every sowing and planting date by the garden's frost dates**, not only tender plant-outs. Potatoes key off the last frost (plant about 1–2 weeks before it), hardy direct sowings key off soil warmth, and nothing goes outside before about 6–8 weeks before the last frost. With my 5 June, potatoes become late May and broad beans mid-April. Test it against a Highland postcode.
2. **Correct the climate for height and let me say "frost hollow"**: lapse the station temperatures by about 0.6 °C per 100 m, estimate the first frost and season length from that, and add a "frost pocket: about 2 weeks colder each end" switch. Aviemore should come out with the first frost in early September and a season of about 180 days, not 5 October and 280.
3. **Fix the season photo covering the planting bar** (and the October title covering the Today ··· menu). Then check every clickable on Garden and Today at 1366 × 768 with `elementFromPoint`.
4. **Make the jobs and cards agree with what I've recorded.** A planted crop drops its "PLANT" job, kale and leeks stay harvestable through winter as their own cards say, and "ready to pick" matches "ready from".
5. **Export my dates as CSV (crop, bed, sown, planted out, first pick, finished) and give a printable plan**, then let next year's plan start from last year's real dates, not the app's defaults.
